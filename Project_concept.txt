Logistic regression specs:

1. Statistical Features

These capture the "shape" and distribution of the baby's movements (e.g., how much they move, in which direction).

    Mean (_mean): The average value of the signal.

        Utility: Tells you the "bias" of the movement (e.g., does the baby tend to keep their head rotated to the left on average?).

    Standard Deviation (_std): The spread of the signal.

        Utility: A direct proxy for stability vs. agitation. A high std in head rotation means the baby is moving their head a lot; low std means they are still.

    Min / Max / Peak-to-Peak (_min, _max, _peak2peak): The extreme values and the total range.

        Utility: Measures the magnitude of the strongest reaction (e.g., the widest smile or the furthest head turn).

    Percentiles (25th, 50th, 75th) (_p25, _p50, _p75): The values below which a percentage of observations fall.

        Utility: robust descriptors of the distribution that are less sensitive to outliers than mean/min/max.

    Skewness (_skew): The asymmetry of the distribution.

        Utility: Detects directional bias. For example, if the baby quickly looks left and slowly returns, the signal will be skewed.

    Kurtosis (_kurtosis): The "tailedness" of the distribution.

        Utility: Distinguishes between sudden jerks (high kurtosis, many outliers) and constant, smooth movement (low kurtosis).

2. Temporal / Dynamics Features

These capture how the movement evolves over time (e.g., speed, rhythm, trends).

    Autocorrelation (Lag 1 & 5) (_auto_lag1, _auto_lag5): How correlated the signal is with a delayed version of itself.

        Utility: Measures smoothness and predictability. High autocorrelation means the baby moves slowly and deliberately; low autocorrelation implies jittery or erratic motion.

    Mean Crossing Rate (_mean_cross): How often the signal crosses its own average.

        Utility: A proxy for frequency. A high crossing rate indicates rapid oscillation (shaking), while a low rate indicates a steady pose.

    Zero Crossing Rate (_zero_cross): How often the signal crosses the value 0.

        Utility: Similar to mean crossing, but specific to the absolute zero baseline (useful for normalized signals like Gaze coordinates).

    Slope (_slope): The angle of a linear regression line fitted to the signal.

        Utility: Captures long-term trends. (e.g., Is the baby slowly turning their head away from the stimulus throughout the entire trial?).

    Absolute Energy (_abs_energy): The sum of squared values.

        Utility: Measures the total intensity or "power" of the movement sequence.

3. Complexity Features

These capture the "texture" of the signal.

    CID_CE (Complexity-Invariant Distance Complexity Estimate) (_cid_ce): Calculated as ∑(xi​−xi−1​)2​.

        Utility: Measures the "jaggedness" or total length of the signal path. A baby who is trembling or making micro-expressions will have a higher complexity score than one making a single smooth movement, even if the range (min/max) is the same.
